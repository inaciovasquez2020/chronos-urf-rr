Let $G=(V,E)$ be a finite graph with $|V|=n$, and let
$f:V\to\{0,1\}$ have odd parity.  Let $TS(G,f)$ be the associated
Tseitin contradiction.  Partition the edge variables arbitrarily as
\[
E=E_A\sqcup E_B,
\]
with Alice receiving the assignment on $E_A$ and Bob the assignment on
$E_B$.

\section*{Reduction to Universal Relation}

For a total edge assignment $x$, define the two vertex strings
$a,b\in\{0,1\}^{V}$ by
\[
a_v
:=
\bigoplus_{\substack{e\in E_A\\ e\ni v}} x_e,
\qquad
b_v
:=
f(v)\oplus
\bigoplus_{\substack{e\in E_B\\ e\ni v}} x_e.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Raw Tseitin search is a Universal-Relation instance]
A vertex $v$ violates its Tseitin parity constraint if and only if
\[
a_v\ne b_v.
\]
Moreover $a\ne b$ for every total edge assignment.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The Tseitin constraint at $v$ is
\[
\bigoplus_{e\ni v}x_e=f(v).
\]
Splitting the incident edges between $E_A$ and $E_B$ shows that this
constraint fails exactly when $a_v\ne b_v$.

It remains to prove that some such vertex always exists.  XOR over all
vertices.  Every edge variable appears exactly twice in the total incidence
sum, so all edge contributions cancel.  Therefore
\[
\bigoplus_{v\in V}(a_v\oplus b_v)
=
\bigoplus_{v\in V} f(v)
=1.
\]
Hence $a$ and $b$ differ in at least one coordinate.
Thus finding a violated Tseitin vertex is exactly an instance of the
Universal Relation: given distinct Boolean strings, find a coordinate on
which they differ.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Randomized communication upper bound]
The public-coin randomized communication complexity of raw edge-partition
Tseitin search is polylogarithmic in $n$; in particular the standard
Universal-Relation protocol gives
\[
R^{\mathrm{pub}}(\mathrm{Search}_{TS(G,f)})
\le O(\log^2 n).
\]
If $TS(G,f)$ is expanded into constant-width CNF on a constant-degree graph,
then after a violated vertex is found only $O(1)$ additional communication is
needed to identify a violated local CNF clause.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Information-complexity consequence]
For any input distribution $\mu$, any transcript-information complexity
obtained by infimizing over such randomized protocols satisfies
\[
IC_\mu(\mathrm{Search}_{TS(G,f)})
\le O(\log^2 n).
\]
\end{corollary}

\section*{Audit boundary}

The previously stated claim
\[
IC_\mu(\mathrm{Search}_{TS(G,f)})=\Omega(n)
\]
for raw Tseitin search under an arbitrary balanced edge partition and a
uniform product distribution is therefore false under the standard
randomized information-complexity interpretation.  Expansion and balance do
not repair this raw model: the reduction above works for every graph and every
edge partition.

Known strong communication lower bounds for Tseitin in proof complexity use
an appropriate \emph{lifted} communication problem or another strengthened
search model.  Such a lifted theorem must be stated with its gadget,
distribution, and communication model explicitly; it cannot be substituted
for the raw edge-partition problem treated here.

No resolution-size lower bound, P vs NP conclusion, or Clay-problem closure is
claimed by this note.
