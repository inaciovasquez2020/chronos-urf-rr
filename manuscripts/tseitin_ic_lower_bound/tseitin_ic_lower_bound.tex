\section*{Certified Scope}

Let $G$ be a sufficiently strong constant-degree expander on $n$ vertices,
and let $\ell:V(G)\to\{0,1\}$ have odd total parity.  Write
$\mathrm{Search}_{\mathrm{Tse}(G,\ell)}$ for the associated falsified-constraint
search problem.

The unlifted claim previously stated in this manuscript,
\[
IC_\mu(\mathrm{Search}_{\mathrm{Tse}(G,\ell)})=\Omega(n),
\]
for an arbitrary balanced edge partition and product distribution $\mu$, is
not established by the critical-block-sensitivity literature and is therefore
withdrawn.

\section*{Known Lifted Communication Lower Bound}

Let $\mathrm{VER}:\mathbb Z_4\times\mathbb Z_4\to\{0,1\}$ denote the constant-size
versatile gadget used in the communication lifting theorem.

\begin{theorem}[G\"o\"os--Pitassi, lifted Tseitin consequence]
There exists a constant-degree graph $G$ on $n$ vertices such that, for every
odd labelling $\ell$ and every partition $(X,Y)$ of the variables, any
bounded-error randomized two-party communication protocol solving
\[
\mathrm{Search}_{X,Y}
\bigl(\mathrm{Tse}(G,\ell)\circ \mathrm{VER}^{|E(G)|}\bigr)
\]
requires
\[
\Omega\!\left(\frac{n}{\log n}\right)
\]
bits of communication.
\end{theorem}

The route is via critical block sensitivity: sufficiently strong
constant-degree expanders satisfy
\[
\operatorname{cbs}(\mathrm{Search}_{\mathrm{Tse}(G,\ell)})
=\Omega\!\left(\frac{n}{\log n}\right),
\]
and the two-party gadget lift preserves this as a randomized communication
lower bound up to constants.

\begin{remark}[Information-complexity boundary]
A lower bound on randomized communication complexity does not, by itself,
imply the same lower bound on information complexity, since information cost
is upper bounded by communication cost.  Any theorem asserting
\[
IC_\mu(\mathrm{Search}_{\mathrm{Tse}}\circ \mathrm{VER}^{|E|})
=\Omega(n/\log n)
\]
therefore requires a separate information-complexity argument and is not
claimed here.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Resolution-size boundary]
This lifted communication lower bound is not yet a DAG-like resolution-size
lower bound.  A separate DAG-compatible lifting or transfer theorem is needed
to convert the communication lower bound into a lower bound on general
resolution length.
\end{remark}
