\section*{Reduction}

Let $F$ be an unsatisfiable CNF and
$\mathrm{Search}_F$ its associated falsified-clause search problem.
Fix a partition of the variables between two parties and a distribution $\mu$
on total assignments.

Let $\pi$ be a resolution refutation of $F$ with length $L(\pi)$ and
clause space $S(\pi)$.

\begin{theorem}[Space-sensitive proof-to-communication reduction]
The transcript-information complexity of the search problem satisfies
\[
IC_\mu(\mathrm{Search}_F)
\le O\!\left(S(\pi)\log L(\pi)\right).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Reduction sketch]
Use the standard space-sensitive simulation of a resolution refutation by a
deterministic two-party protocol for the falsified-clause search problem.
A length-$L$ refutation executable in clause space $S$ yields a protocol of
communication cost $O(S\log L)$ (and $O(\log L)$ rounds).
For any input distribution $\mu$, transcript information is at most transcript
entropy, which in turn is at most the worst-case number of communicated bits.
Therefore
\[
IC_\mu(\mathrm{Search}_F)
\le CC(\mathrm{Search}_F)
\le O(S(\pi)\log L(\pi)).
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Length--space consequence]
Every resolution refutation $\pi$ satisfies
\[
S(\pi)\log L(\pi)
\ge \Omega\!\left(IC_\mu(\mathrm{Search}_F)\right).
\]
In particular, when $S(\pi)>0$,
\[
L(\pi)
\ge
2^{\Omega\!\left(IC_\mu(\mathrm{Search}_F)/S(\pi)\right)}.
\]
\end{corollary}

\section*{Audit boundary}

The stronger size-only implication
\[
IC_\mu(\mathrm{Search}_F)\le O(\log L(F))
\]
does not follow from general DAG-like resolution merely by ``balancing the
DAG.''  Resolution size and depth can exhibit nontrivial tradeoffs, so the
space factor cannot be silently discarded in the general reduction.

Consequently, even a linear information-complexity lower bound
$IC_\mu(\mathrm{Search}_F)=\Omega(n)$ gives only
\[
L(\pi)\ge 2^{\Omega(n/S(\pi))}
\]
through this route.  An exponential resolution-length lower bound therefore
requires an additional suitable upper bound on the relevant proof space, or a
different reduction that genuinely controls length without this space loss.
No such additional theorem is claimed here.
